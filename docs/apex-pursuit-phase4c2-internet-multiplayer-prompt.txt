APEX PURSUIT — PHASE 4C.2 PROMPT: ONLINE RACES WITH FRIENDS AT THEIR OWN HOMES
===========================================================================

Goal
----
Me and my friends each play from our own homes, on our own Wi-Fi. They run the game from the
GitHub ZIP (play/ApexPursuit.html). Example session: me + 2 friends (later 3) join one
PURSUIT race, rain, dusk, 3 laps. Humans take grid slots; AI rivals fill the rest of the grid
of 8 as usual; the police chase everyone.

What exists today (Phase 4C)
----------------------------
- OnlineSession: up to 4 humans share a grid of 8; AI fills the remaining slots. The first
  player in is the HOST: runs the AI rivals, the police (pursuit), the grid and the start
  lights, and sends weather, time of day, rain strength and laps to everyone. A player joining
  sends everyone back to the grid; if the host leaves, the next player takes over.
- Transports: "relay" (WebSocket to server/relay.mjs: works only when the page is opened from
  the relay's own address), "room" (the shared claude.ai page), "tabs" (same browser).
- The problem: the relay runs on one PC and is only reachable on that PC's local network, and
  the ZIP's HTML file (file://) has no way to connect to a relay at all.

Plan
----
1. JOIN BY ADDRESS. Online menu in the game: "Host" / "Join". Join takes a server address
   (paste a link like https://xxxx.trycloudflare.com/?room=friends, or a short code) and a
   room name. The ZIP's HTML file can then connect to any relay over wss://. Remember the last
   address. Clear errors: can't reach server, room full (4 humans), version mismatch.
2. HOSTING FROM HOME, NO ROUTER SETUP. "Host online race.bat" (and host-online.sh) gains an
   internet option: it starts the relay and a free Cloudflare quick tunnel (cloudflared,
   downloaded once from Cloudflare's official GitHub releases, with the user's OK), then prints
   and copies a public https link. The host sends that link to friends (WhatsApp etc.).
   Friends either paste it into Join, or just open the link in Chrome / Edge.
   Fallbacks, documented: Tailscale / ZeroTier (private network), router port forwarding, or a
   free always-on host for the relay (e.g. Render / Fly.io) so nobody has to host.
3. LOBBY. Before the race: player list with names, paint and ping; host picks mode (race /
   pursuit), weather (incl. rain strength), time of day and laps; "Ready" for each player;
   host presses Start. Late joiners wait in the lobby instead of resetting a running race
   (change from today's "joining resets to the grid").
4. GRID: humans first in join order, AI fills to 8 (Settings → online AI count 0–7). If a
   human drops mid-race, their car is taken over by an AI driver ("replaced by NPC") instead
   of vanishing; if they rejoin, they get it back at the next race.
5. PURSUIT ONLINE: police target every human fairly (heat per player), busted/escaped per
   player, results screen for all. Police and helicopter stay host-simulated.
6. NETWORK QUALITY over the internet (100–250 ms between countries): interpolation buffer
   tuned for higher ping, extrapolation with snap correction, 20 Hz states, message size kept
   small, ping shown in the lobby and the leaderboard, host migration that keeps the race
   going if possible.
7. SECURITY / FAIR PLAY (friendly level): room names, optional room password, rate limits and
   size limits on the relay (already partly there), validation of every message (already).
8. TESTS: tools/nettest.py extended: 3 clients through the relay with simulated 150 ms latency
   and loss, a pursuit race to the finish, a player dropping and AI taking over, a late joiner
   waiting in the lobby. A real test over the internet through a tunnel.
9. README "Play online with friends at home" section: step by step for the host and for
   friends, with screenshots of the lobby.

Constraints: no real brands; my email stays private; don't break solo play or the claude.ai
page; commit to github.com/Desin898/apex-pursuit and refresh play/ApexPursuit.html.
