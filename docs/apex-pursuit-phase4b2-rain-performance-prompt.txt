PART 1: HEAVY RAIN WITH REALISTIC LENS DROPS
1. Rain intensity
- Add a rain intensity from 0 to 1 to the weather. Light rain is about 0.3, rain about 0.6 and a downpour 1.0.
- In a race it changes on its own, slowly, over minutes: showers build into downpours and ease off again.
  - The host decides it, and online guests get the same value.
  - A setting gives "Rain strength: Changing / Light / Heavy / Downpour".
- Intensity drives everything together: streak count and brightness, splash count, spray amount, road wetness and puddle shine, fog density, how fast lens drops gather, and the windscreen rain in the cockpit.
- At 0.8 and above the chase camera must look as heavy as Image 2:
  - dense streaks across the whole view, still lit by lamps and car lights but visible in the ambient light too
  - longer streaks than now, but still capped so they never become a hyperspace tunnel

2. Lens drops (outside views)
- Turn them back on by default, and make their amount follow the rain intensity. A dry or light shower gives a few; a downpour gives Image 2 levels. The setting becomes "Lens drops: Auto / Off".
- What makes them realistic:
  - Each drop is a small lens. It shows the scene behind it shrunk and flipped, slightly blurred, with a soft bright rim on the side facing the light. No dark outlines and no hollow rings.
  - Size varies: many small beads, some medium drops, a few large ones.
  - Drops build up over a few seconds as rain lands, and new drops appear at random places. When two drops overlap they merge into a bigger one.
  - Gravity: once a drop is big enough it slides DOWN the lens slowly, wobbling. It leaves a thin wet trail that fades, and it collects smaller beads on the way.
  - NO sideways running at speed. Speed only does two things: drops arrive faster (the car drives into the rain), and the biggest drops on the edges of the lens may tremble or slowly slide outwards, never streak across the screen.
  - Drops in front of bright lights (lamps, tail lights, police bars) glow, with a soft bokeh bloom.
  - They dry or blow off slowly once the rain stops, over about 10 s, not instantly.
- Brief clearing moments add life: a big gust (or nitrous) can flick a few drops off the lens.
- Keep a hard limit so the centre of the screen (where the road is) never gets more than a light covering. Heavy drops gather more at the edges and corners, like a real camera.
- Medium: one layer of drops at half resolution. High and Ultra: two layers. Low: no lens drops.

3. Cockpit
- Keep the windscreen rain from Image 1. Link its amount to the rain intensity too, and speed up the wiper in a downpour (two speeds plus intermittent in light rain).

PART 2: SMOOTH 60 FPS ON AN RTX 3050 LAPTOP
In Images 1 and 3 the frame takes 22–23 ms while the GPU needs only 12–17 ms. So the main thread (JavaScript work, draw-call submission) is the bottleneck, not just the graphics card. Find the real cost first, then fix it.

1. Measure
- Extend the F3 overlay with a CPU breakdown, in ms per frame:
  - physics steps
  - AI and police
  - particles
  - rain and environment update
  - HUD and canvases
  - scene update (matrices)
  - render submission (main, wet-road mirror, paint probe, cockpit mirrors)
  - post-processing
- Also show draw calls per pass.
- Add a benchmark mode (F4) that flies a fixed camera path round the track for 30 s in pursuit, night and heavy rain. It reports average fps, 1% low fps, average CPU ms and GPU ms, and copies the result to the clipboard.

2. Fix what the measurement shows. Likely candidates:
- Particles: move the smoke, spray and mist simulation onto the GPU, like the rain (spawn data in a ring buffer, motion computed in the vertex shader), so the CPU only writes new particles.
- Draw calls:
  - merge each police car into a few meshes (one per material) and instance the identical parts (wheels, light modules)
  - make sure static batching covers all roadside props
  - cull small props by distance
- Extra renders:
  - the wet-road mirror at a lower resolution and only every second frame on High, skipping props and particles
  - the paint probe at one face every 2 frames
  - cockpit mirrors at 30 Hz, low detail (no particles, no small props) and a short far plane
- Physics: keep 120 Hz, but skip AI-car sub-steps when a car is far from the camera and not in contact, using a simpler integration, while staying deterministic for online.
- HUD: redraw the canvases only when values change, at most 30 Hz for the minimap.
- Avoid per-frame garbage (temporary vectors, arrays, closures) in the hot paths.

3. Keep it smooth automatically
- Dynamic resolution: when the frame time goes over 16.7 ms, lower the render scale in small steps (down to 70%), and raise it again when there is headroom. On by default ("Target 60 fps").
- A frame-rate setting: 60 / 120 / Unlimited.
- Choose the GPU properly:
  - Keep powerPreference: 'high-performance'.
  - In the graphics settings, show which GPU the browser reports (WEBGL_debug_renderer_info). If it is an integrated GPU, show a tip: set the browser or Claude app to "High performance" in Windows Graphics settings, and plug in the charger.

4. Targets
- RTX 3050 laptop, 1080p, plugged in:
  - High: 60 fps average and a 1% low above 50 in the benchmark, in night, heavy rain, Heat 5
  - Ultra: 45+ fps
  - Cockpit view on High: 60 fps
- Report the before and after F3 numbers in the README.
